Yono App Permissions: What's Normal and What's a Red Flag

By All Yono Official Editorial Team · App Safety

Every app on this site installs as a direct APK rather than through the Play Store, which means none of them go through Google's own permission review before reaching your phone. That makes it worth knowing, in plain terms, what a card-game app actually needs to function — and which permission requests go beyond that and deserve a second look before you tap "Allow."

Why This Matters More for Sideloaded APKs

A Play Store app is scanned and its permission requests are visible before install, with Google able to flag abusive patterns. A sideloaded APK skips that layer entirely — the only check happening is whatever you do yourself, at install time and afterward. That doesn't make every sideloaded app unsafe, but it does mean the usual "it's on the Play Store so it's probably fine" shortcut doesn't apply here.

Common Permissions and What They're Normally For

PermissionTypical Legitimate UseWorth a Second Look If...
Install unknown appsRequired just to install the APK itself (one-time, during setup)It's still enabled site-wide after you're done installing
StorageSaving the downloaded install file, caching game assetsIt requests access to all files rather than just its own folder
Internet / network stateConnecting to game servers, loading promo-code dataThis one is expected for essentially any online app
SMSAuto-reading an OTP during sign-up, on some appsIt's requested outside of a sign-up flow, or kept long after setup
ContactsNo typical legitimate use for a card gameRequested at all — there's rarely a real reason a rummy or Teen Patti app needs your contact list
Display over other apps / overlayRare, legitimate use is a floating in-game widgetCombined with other sensitive permissions, since overlays can be used to mimic login screens
Accessibility serviceEssentially never needed by a card gameRequested at all — this permission can read and control what's on your screen

How to Check What Permissions an App Already Has

On most Android phones: open Settings, go to Apps, select the app in question, then Permissions. This shows every permission currently granted and lets you turn off anything that looks unnecessary without uninstalling the app. It's worth doing this check once after installing any app from this category, not just the one you're about to install next.

What All Yono Official Does and Doesn't Verify

Final Note

Most of the permissions these apps request are ordinary and tied to how a sideloaded game actually works. The exceptions — contacts, accessibility service, or SMS access outside a sign-up flow — are the ones worth pausing on before you tap Allow, regardless of which specific app is asking.
